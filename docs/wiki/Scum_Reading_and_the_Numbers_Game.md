# Scum Reading and the Numbers Game

Fuente: https://town-of-salem.fandom.com/wiki/Scum_Reading_and_the_Numbers_Game

Scum reading is an important skill to have. The numbers game is also important, but is only useful to roles like the Vigilante, Jailor, and Mayor to catch evils by surprise who do not have a fake claim ready.

Scum Reading[]

What is scum reading?[]

Scum reading is the ability to analyze someone's actions and claims, including their writing structure and patterns of speech, to determine if they are evil. For example:

The Death Note that the Mafia have been writing is written in perfect prose. Apart from the Vigilante, one person talks in terrible prose and one person talks in perfect prose. The Vigilante shoots the person with perfect prose, and they are the Mafioso.

Telling the difference between a real Veteran baiting and a fake one trying to look like a Veteran is scum reading.

Check a Death Note’s grammar: Perfect grammar in a Death Note most likely reveals that the killer uses perfect grammar.

Note the usage of other typography, such as emoticons, etc.

People who "try too hard" to act like a Townie, such as trying to help out and talking frequently but never actually giving information on who is evil, could be an evil trying to pretend to be a Townie.

 Mafia members calling out Sheriffs as Executioners when they call out a member of the Mafia as suspicious.

Completely silent people are more likely to be evil players to not draw attention to themselves, but could also be important Town roles.

Other common traits of Mafia players are confidence and trying too hard to help the Town with Neutral Killings, but becoming lack-luster when going after the Mafia.

This ability can come naturally to people who have played a lot of games, and allows them to find potential evils simply by just how they act. If an experienced scum reader is a Jailor or Investigator, you may be able to confirm your suspicions.

The primary weakness of scum reading is how evils may bluff, and attempt to frame another Townie, or be unrecognizable in their written voice.

Why is it important?[]

Scum reading is valuable to the Town because it provides an alternate avenue to discerning evil roles. Without scum reading, all investigation/interrogation is based on a seemingly "random" click of a mouse. With scum reading, however, a Town Investigative is able to confirm their suspicions about a target acting strangely.

Strategy[]

If a Mafioso was killed and you see someone who either hasn't claimed or hasn't spoken all game say something along the lines of: "YAY!", you can assume that they are an evil pretending to be helpful. You can also check the chat log and see who didn't vote on the Mafioso (if they were hanged) to help get more proof.

You can also tell by how people react when you whisper them for a role; if they refuse to claim to you (either by ignoring you or giving excuses for not claiming), they have a higher chance of being evil, but if they give you a Last Will that has a lot of information (or they claim an easily confirmable Town role that evils almost never claim, like Mayor), then they are most likely Town. This is especially true if the person asking for claims is a confirmed Town, as there is almost no reason for an actual Townie to not claim to them.

Common evil claims like Sheriff, Retributionist, or Bodyguard shouldn't be trusted as much as easily confirmable Town claims like Transporter or Jailor. Of course, you shouldn't automatically believe any claim, but other scum reading strategies can help you with this.

Ranked Strategy[]

Scum reading is a fundamental part of all matches, including Ranked. Ranked is a competitive mode with mostly skilled players, and if you want to match their skill you should learn the basics of scum reading.

Branching off of whispers[]

If an evil is killed, you should check their whispers and see if they defended anyone or if they brought up false information to protect someone. If they did any of these things, the player they whispered with might be evil as well. If they are a Witch, the first thing you should do is check who they whispered to; the Witch might have found members of the Mafia and tried to ally with them. It's usually best to find who claimed to be bewitched, and who didn't after being whispered to; evils usually don't claim to be bewitched because they don't want to reveal their ally to the Town. These small things can help you find evils more easily, but don't get too carried away and throw accusations every time you see a slightly suspicious action.

Benefits of practicing[]

Scum reading is important to the Town, even during a loss. Use the information you gained from last game to become more experienced the more you play. If you mis-hanged someone because of incorrect scum reading, you might need to change your strategy, but if it is a successful hang, you can catch evils that will act a similar way in the future.

How they act in Jail[]

If a jailee takes a long time to claim, then they could be an evil trying to think of a reasonable role.

Additionally, if they claim a role that they could've counterclaimed someone else with earlier, then they're probably evil. For example: the jailee claimed Doctor, but the day before, someone claimed Bodyguard and there were no counterclaims against him.

Saying things such as "Hello", "wassssssup", or "huh", is a sign of an evil trying to buy time to think of a role, or one trying to act natural.

You can see if a player is trustworthy or not if an unconfirmed role starts accusing someone, and they turn out to be a member of the Mafia. The accuser then is probably a Townie, so it is in the Town's best interest to keep them alive and listen to their future accusations.

 Last Will Changing[]

Trying to find potentially false Last Wills is an important part of scum reading. If you think someone is suspicious and they claim a role that should always have a Last Will, such as Sheriff, then check the Chat Log to see how often they post their Last Will. A Sheriff claim that doesn't post a Last Will or posts two contradictory Last Wills is definitely a fake claim. Remember, that people who do this might be Jesters (and, in general, many seemingly obvious evils that you scum read may be Jesters as well.)

The Numbers Game[]

What is the numbers game?[]

If there are more evils than Townies, or evils are about to gain majority, then any Jailors or Vigilantes should start to shoot or execute others without much proof, because their team is certainly doomed and will be hanged by the evil majority otherwise. If they fail, then the evils win anyway, but if they succeed, it gives the Town a chance. If there's an evil majority, they have a better chance of hitting another evil (though the Vigilante should try and shoot roles with no Defense to not waste a night). Scum reading can help you have a higher chance at killing the right people.

Scum Reading as an Evil Role[]

Scum reading can also be applied as an evil role, used primarily to pinpoint high-priority Town roles and pick them off before they can become too prominent.

The Jailor when talking to the jailee often misspells their words. Based on conversations during the day, look for people who misspell words often over those who correctly spell everything. Targeting the people who misspell could result in you killing the Jailor.

Pinpointing who is a Veteran versus a Vigilante who is acting like one could be utilized to kill a lethal Town Killing role early on, and avoid falling into a trap.

Aggressive people early in the game during the day tend to be a Veteran, while signs they may instead be a Vigilante faking it could point to obvious Veteran baits or general suspicion.

Someone who immediately whispers to a Jailor who claimed Day 1 could be a Lookout, Spy, or a Town Protective role.

This also applies to people who claim they were healed, as someone who whispers them could be a Doctor.

People who seem to be "leading the Town", such as providing information and whispering others for their claim, could be an important Town role, like a Mayor.

Anyone who claims to be a Witch to you by whispering, without being controlled prior, could be a Town role trying to get you to reveal your fellow members. They could also be a Jester, but your best bet would be to immediately announce their whisper during the day to throw attention on them, or kill them during the night.

Completely silent people are more likely to be evil players to not draw attention to themselves, but could also be important Town roles.

Other common traits of an important Town role are voting innocent on a Jester when there's a Neutral Evil slot still open.

This ability can come naturally to people who have played a lot of games, and allows them to find hidden Jailors or Vigilantes simply by just how they act. If an experienced Town reader is a Consigliere or Potion Master, you may be able to confirm your suspicions.

The primary weakness of scum reading is that some Townies like a Medium may be doing these actions instead to throw evil roles off, and Town Protective roles can spot these too and protect those targets.

Sheeping[]

What is Sheeping?[]

Sheeping is the ability to persuade another player into doing certain actions to gain an advantage. Any evil can do it, but the Mafia has the easiest time doing so, due to the ability to easily fake another Town role and change the Town's information.

One of the most notable examples is convincing a revealed Mayor to vote for someone with a suspicious claim (e.g. a Doctor claim while a Serial Killer is alive).

Why is it important?[]

Sheeping is valuable to evildoers because it provides an alternate avenue to simply attacking Townies. Without sheeping, the Town can very easily deduce who is evil and who is innocent, then perform the correct actions. With sheeping, however, a role such as Blackmailer or Coven Leader is able to persuade a Townie into doing certain actions for their benefit. Evil roles can also draw attention to other players, for instance, one may whisper a Jailor to jail a "suspicious" target, or make a Town Protective role protect someone else. Executioners often rely on this as one of their primary paths in order to win

Strategy[]

Asking a Transporter to transport certain (or random) players can give you a free attack on the Transporter. If you're a role that attacks visitors (such as an Ambusher or Medusa), you can even threaten them to bait them into transporting themselves with you.

Asking a Town Protective to be on you can grant you free protection away from other Townies and can bait other killing roles into attacking you. Of course, you will need to become a "proven" Townie for this to work; simply claiming Sheriff and accusing a Serial Killer or bussing a fellow member can work.

In a plurality, acting suspicious as the Godfather can bait a Vigilante or Serial Killer into attacking you instead of a fellow member.

 Ranked Strategy[]

Sheeping is a fundamental part of all matches, including Ranked, which is a competitive mode with mostly skilled players, and if you want to match their skill you should learn the basics of sheeping.

Playing whisper games[]

You can whisper to your team or an ally pertinent information while whispering to Townies asking for their role and/or Last Will to make other Townies believe you're simply asking for claims. It is easier to do this as a Blackmailer, as you can see all whispers and act accordingly.

If you find a Vigilante, you can whisper them to shoot a certain person, such as a hidden Mayor or Jailor. Of course, you will need to have demonstrated that you are trustable for this to work.

Whispering to a Jailor pointing out someone who is silent can lead them into jailing them, at which point you can whisper to a Witch to control them into executing their prisoner. If you're the Witch/ Coven Leader yourself, it's even better as you don't need to potentially expose yourself by whispering to your ally.

Benefits of practicing[]

Sheeping is important to evildoers, even during a loss. Use the information you gained from last game to become more experienced the more you play. If you get killed because of incorrect sheeping, you might need to change your strategy, but if you successfully convince others, you can control an entire Town in the future (better than a Witch).

In a 1 v 1 v 1[]

Sometimes, you may need to claim to be a Neutral Killing role to convince a Townie or Survivor into hanging the real Neutral Killing role. This also works the other way around.

In addition, you can still stick to your original claim if a Townie trusts that you're a "confirmed Townie", as revealing your role unnecessarily can confuse the Townie and lead them into hanging you.

Using flaws in a Last Will[]

Pointing out flaws in someone's Last Will is also an important tool for sheeping. If you see someone post their Last Will and they have a flaw in their Last Will (e.g. a Sheriff who claims they interrogated themselves due to a typo), call them out to make the Town lose trust in their claim and potentially hang them. As a Jester, you can also do this to someone with a normal Last Will to raise suspicion on yourself.
